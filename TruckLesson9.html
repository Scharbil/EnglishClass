Reading Section

After finishing the delivery, the driver noticed a sudden vibration in the steering wheel. Suddenly, a loud bang echoed through the cab—a Blowout. He carefully steered the tanker onto the Shoulder of the highway to assess the damage. Putting on his Safety Vest, he stepped out and saw the rear outer tire was completely Flat.

Safety was the first priority. He walked fifty yards behind the truck to place a Safety Triangle to warn oncoming traffic. Returning to the wheel, he checked the Tread of the remaining tires and noticed they were still in good condition, but the damaged one was shredded. He immediately placed Wheel Chocks under the front tires to ensure the tanker wouldn't move during the repair.

He grabbed the Hydraulic Jack from the storage compartment and positioned it under the axle. Before lifting the heavy vehicle, he used a Torque Wrench to loosen the Lug Nuts, which were tightened extremely hard. It took a lot of leverage, but eventually, they began to turn.

With the Lug Nuts removed and the truck lifted, he swapped the damaged tire for the spare. After lowering the jack, he did a final check to ensure everything was secure. "It’s a delay," he muttered, "but at least I have the right tools to get back on the road safely."

Once the tools were put away, he performed a quick walk-around Inspection. He made sure the Spare Tire pressure was adequate and that no other debris from the Blowout had damaged the brake lines. Satisfied, he climbed back into the cab, checked his mirrors, and carefully merged back into traffic to complete his journey to the depot.

4. Final Discussion

  • Why is it essential to steer the tanker onto the Shoulder immediately after a Blowout?
  • What is the specific safety purpose of using Wheel Chocks before lifting the truck with a Hydraulic Jack?
  • How does placing a Safety Triangle fifty yards behind the vehicle protect the driver while they work?
  • Why must a Torque Wrench be used to loosen the Lug Nuts instead of a standard small wrench?
  • How can checking the Tread during a post-incident Inspection help prevent future mechanical failures?
