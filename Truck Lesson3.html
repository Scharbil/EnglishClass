Reading Section

Mark was driving his 18-wheeler through a quiet stretch of the interstate when he noticed flashing blue lights in his side mirror. The officer's voice crackled over the radio: "Driver of the blue tractor-trailer, please pull over to the right shoulder immediately and turn off your engine." Mark signaled, slowed down, and carefully parked on the gravel.

As the officer approached the cab, Mark rolled down his window and placed his hands on the steering wheel where they could be seen. "Good afternoon, Officer," Mark said calmly. The officer nodded and replied, "Good afternoon. Please step out of the cab and provide your CDL, medical certificate, and the vehicle registration."

Mark climbed down and handed over his documents. The officer examined them closely before asking about the trip's details. "What is your origin and what is your final destination for this haul?" the officer inquired. Mark answered, "I'm coming from a warehouse in Chicago and I'm heading to a distribution center in Laredo, Texas."

"I see. And what are you carrying today?" the officer asked. Mark reached into his folder and pulled out a stamped document. "I am hauling industrial machinery, Officer. Here is the Bill of Lading with the cargo description and the weight details from the last weigh station."

The officer then pointed to the tablet inside the cab. "I need to check your ELD (Electronic Logging Device). I want to see your logbook for the last seven days to ensure you are in compliance with the hours of service regulations." Mark synchronized the device and showed the digital logs, which proved he had taken his mandatory rest breaks.

"Everything looks organized so far," the officer noted, "but I noticed you don't have any hazmat placards. Are you carrying any batteries or chemicals?" Mark shook his head, "No, sir. It is strictly non-hazardous machinery. You can check the DOT number on the door; my company has a clean safety record for this type of freight."

The inspection moved to the exterior of the truck. "Please apply the brakes," the officer commanded while standing behind the trailer to check the lights. Afterward, he knelt down with a gauge to measure the tread depth of the tires and inspected each axle for cracks or air leaks. "Safety is our priority on this highway," the officer added.

After twenty minutes, the officer handed the paperwork back to Mark with a signed inspection report. "You've passed the Level 2 inspection, Driver. Your equipment is in great shape and your logs are perfect. Keep up the good work and have a safe trip to Texas." Mark thanked him, climbed back into his seat, and prepared to rejoin the traffic.

4. Discusión Final
