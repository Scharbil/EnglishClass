Reading Scenario

Officer Miller: Good afternoon. Please keep your hands on the steering wheel where I can see them. The reason I asked you to pull over today is that one of your taillights is out, which is a safety hazard on the highway. Additionally, I also noticed you bypassed the last weigh station about five miles back there without stopping for inspection.

Carlos: Good afternoon, Officer. I apologize for the light; I did a pre-trip inspection this morning and everything seemed fine. I truly didn't realize the light was broken. Regarding the weigh station, there were no flashing lights active, so I honestly thought it was closed for the day. I didn't mean to bypass any mandatory checks.

Officer Miller: Every commercial vehicle must verify the signals at the station. For now, may I see your driver’s license, vehicle registration, and proof of insurance? I also need to verify your medical examiner's certificate and your authorization to transport this specific type of freight.

Carlos: Of course, Officer. Let me reach into my folder here. Here is my Class A driver’s license, the registration for both the tractor and the trailer, and the current insurance card. I also have my Bill of Lading ready so you can see exactly what cargo I am hauling and its total weight.

Officer Miller: Thank you. This Bill of Lading shows you are carrying non-hazardous materials, which matches your manifest. However, I need to ensure you are compliant with the Hours of Service regulations. Please provide your electronic logbook or your paper logs if the system is down.

Carlos: Here is the tablet with my logbook, Officer. As you can see, I have been recording all my duty status changes correctly. I took my mandatory 30-minute break two hours ago, and I am well within my driving limits for this 24-hour period. I always try to keep my logbook updated to avoid problems.

Officer Miller: I appreciate your cooperation. Please stay in the cab while I go back to my patrol car to verify these documents. I am going to cross-reference your logbook with the time stamps from the previous weigh station. Do not exit the vehicle until I return.

Carlos: Understood, Officer. I will stay right here. I hope everything is in order with my driver’s license and the rest of the paperwork. I will make sure to call my dispatcher to report the broken light as soon as we finish here.

Officer Miller: Everything looks consistent. If the rest of the check is clear, I will give you a written warning for the broken light instead of a citation. However, you must fix it at the next truck stop to avoid a real citation later. Remember, a citation can affect your company's safety rating.
