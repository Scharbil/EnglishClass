Reading Scenario

Officer Sanchez: Good afternoon. I am conducting a random Level 3 Inspection today. Please keep your hands visible. First, I need to see your CDL and your current Medical Card to ensure you are fit to operate this commercial vehicle.

Luis: Good afternoon, Officer. Not a problem. Here is my Class A CDL and my medical certificate. I keep my Medical Card in this folder along with all my local trip authorization documents.

Officer Sanchez: Thank you. I noticed your DOT Number on the driver-side door is a bit faded and hard to read; you should fix that. Also, please provide your DVIR to confirm that you performed your safety check before leaving the yard this morning.

Luis: Here is the DVIR, Officer. I checked the truck at 6:00 AM. I’ll make sure to repaint the DOT Number as soon as I get back to the terminal. I want to make sure my company stays compliant.

Officer Sanchez: I see you are heading toward the downtown district. Are you aware of the Weight limit on the bypass bridge? Also, let me see your Delivery manifest to check how many stops you have left and what you are carrying.

Luis: Yes, I checked the Weight limit, and I am well under it. Here is my Delivery manifest. Since I am carrying industrial cleaners, I also have my Hazmat permit attached to the paperwork right here.

Officer Sanchez: Everything seems to be in order with the paperwork. However, I need you to step out and check your Air hoses. I can hear a slight "hissing" sound, and an audible air leak is a safety violation. We also need to double-check your Load securement.

Luis: I’ll check the Air hoses immediately, Officer. Regarding the Load securement, I used extra straps this morning to make sure nothing moves during my local deliveries. I take safety very seriously.

Officer Sanchez: Good. If the air leak is minor and you can fix it now, I will let you go. If your Load securement is tight and your documents match, you’ll be on your way. Drive safely.
