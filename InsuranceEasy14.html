Reading Section

Agent: "Thank you for taking my call, Mr. Miller. My goal today is not to just sell a policy, but to understand your specific needs. To provide customized coverage, I need to know a little more about your current situation."

Agent: "Could you start by telling me what your main concerns are regarding your family's protection for the next five years?"

Client: "Well, I'm worried about my mortgage and my children's education."

Agent: "That makes perfect sense. To help me do an accurate asset assessment, could you share your financial priorities? For example, are you more focused on immediate savings or long-term investment?"

Agent: "It is important that you feel understood throughout this process. Based on your answers, I can build a risk profile that allows us to mitigate risks effectively. My value proposition is to give you peace of mind, not just paperwork."

Agent: "Finally, if you had to prioritize one thing in your policy—lower monthly premiums or higher coverage limits—which one would it be?"

4. Final Discussion

  • Why is it better to ask open-ended questions instead of "Yes/No" questions?
  • How do you explain your value proposition without sounding like you are just reading a script?
  • When a client shares their main concerns, how do you acknowledge them professionally?
  • How can a risk profile change your recommendation for a client's coverage?
  • What is the most effective way to help a client feel understood during a long phone call?
